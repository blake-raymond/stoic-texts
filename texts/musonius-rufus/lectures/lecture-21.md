# XXI. On hair

He said that a man should treat his head just as we treat a vine when we prune it, cutting only to remove what is useless; [...] and that the beard should not be cut from the chin, but is also a covering that nature has provided us. The beard has become a mark of the male, like the crest of the cock and the mane of the lion. So what is troublesome should be removed from the hair, but nothing from the beard. For the beard is no trouble, as long as the body is healthy, or the illness is not of the sort for which the removal of the hair on the chin would be the remedy. Zeno's saying was good, he said: that one should cut the hair for the same reason one should also let it grow, namely what is according to nature, so that no one is weighed down by hair, or troubled, or hindered in any activity. For nature appears to protect what is lacking more, and what is superfluous less, in plants and in animals, since the removal of the superfluous is much easier and quicker than the addition of what is lacking. One must help the human reasoning to support nature in both ways, filling the lacks as far as one can, but reducing and removing the excesses. Hence the hair should be cut only in order to remove what is superfluous, and not for adornment: unlike those who think it necessary to smooth the beard, imitating the beardless, or, by Zeus, the newly bearded, and who cut the head unevenly, treating the front differently from the back. This seems to be orderly, but has a great deal of disorder, and does not differ from the primping of women. For women braid some parts of their hair, let some hang down, and arrange the rest in another way, so that they may look prettier. The men who cut their hair this way are conspicuous for their desire to look beautiful to those whom they want to please, removing some hairs altogether and shaping others so as to be most attractive to the women and boys by whom they want to be praised. Some even cut their hair to relieve the weight itself, and smooth the beard, plainly dragged down by luxury and unmanned, who endure to be seen as men-women and womanish, which they should avoid at all costs if they really were men. For what burden are hairs to human beings? Unless, by Zeus, one would say that feathers are a burden to birds.
