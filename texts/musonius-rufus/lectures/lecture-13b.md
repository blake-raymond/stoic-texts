# XIII B. What is the chief end of marriage (continued)

Therefore those who marry must look not to descent, whether the partners come from well-born families, nor to money, whether they possess much, nor to bodies, whether they are beautiful. For neither wealth nor beauty nor noble birth by nature increases the partnership, any more than they increase concord; nor do they make childbearing better. What suffices for marriage is bodies that are healthy, of middling appearance, and able to work with their own hands; such people are less likely to be plotted against by the licentious and will work harder at whatever work the body does, and will not be deficient in producing children. As for the souls, the most suitable are those best naturally disposed toward temperance and justice and in general toward virtue. For what marriage is good without concord? What partnership is useful? How could bad people be of one mind with each other? Or how would a good person agree with a bad one? No more than a straight piece of wood would fit with a crooked one, or two crooked ones with each other. A crooked thing does not fit with a similar crooked thing, and with its opposite, the straight, still less. The bad man is not a friend or in harmony with the bad man, and much less with the good man.
