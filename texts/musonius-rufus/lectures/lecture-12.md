# XII. On sexual matters

A not small part of luxury lies in sexual matters. Those who live luxuriously require a variety of lovers, not only lawful but unlawful ones, not only women but men, hunting now one beloved and now another. They are not content with what is readily at hand, but go after the rare, and seek out disgraceful couplings, all of which are grounds for serious reproach against a human being.

Those who are not luxurious, or not bad, must regard as just only the sexual relations that take place in marriage and for the begetting of children, since these are lawful. Those that are hunted for pleasure alone are unjust and unlawful, even if they occur in marriage. Of other unions, adulterous ones are the most unlawful, and unions with males are no more moderate than these, because the act is contrary to nature. All unions with women outside adultery that are deprived of lawful procreation are likewise shameful; they are carried out out of licentiousness. So that, with temperance, no one would endure to approach a courtesan, nor, by Zeus, a free woman without marriage, nor even his own female slave. That these unions are neither lawful nor decent is a shame and a great reproach to those who hunt them. Hence none of these people allows himself to do any of this openly, even for a short blush; they do it concealed and in secret, those at least who are not completely lost. And yet to try to hide what one is doing is an admission of error.

"By Zeus," someone says, "but the man who corrupts another's wife wrongs her husband, whereas the man who consorts with a courtesan wrongs no one, and neither does the man with a woman who has no husband; for he destroys no one's hope of children." I refrain from saying that everyone who errs does wrong at once, even if to none of his neighbours, certainly to himself, since he shows himself worse and less honourable; for the one who errs is worse and less honourable by exactly as much as he errs. But I leave injustice aside; licentiousness, at least, must certainly attach to anyone who is mastered by shameful pleasure and delights in being befouled, like swine. Of this kind, in no small part, is also the man who approaches his own slave woman, which some think the most blameless of all, since every master seems free to use his slave as he likes. To this my argument is simple. If it seems to someone neither shameful nor absurd for a master to approach his female slave, especially if she happens to be a widow, let him consider how it would seem to him if a mistress approached her male slave. Would it not seem intolerable, not only if a woman who has a lawful husband took a slave, but even if she did this being unmarried? And yet no one would think men worse than women, or less able to govern their desires, the stronger in judgment than the weaker, the rulers than the ruled. Men must be far better, if they claim to be in charge of women. If, then, they are shown to be less self-controlled and worse... That approaching a female slave is the work of lack of self-control and nothing else, what need is there to say? It is well known.
