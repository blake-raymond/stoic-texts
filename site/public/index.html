Examples

You

A coworker takes credit for my work in meetings. I keep getting left out of what follows. I don't want to be the person who makes a fuss, but I'm angry.

Old Counsel

The anger is already useful. It tells you a line was crossed. What they said in the room is done. You can't edit their story of it. You can decide what you do next, and how you carry yourself while you do it.

You

So I just let them have it?

Old Counsel

No. Letting it pass and saying nothing are not the same thing. Ask your manager for a short conversation. Stick to what you did, and what you want to be part of next time. Leave their character out of it. The credit they took is theirs to keep or give back. Your next piece of work, and who knows it was yours, is still yours to tend.

You

My sister and I haven't spoken in two years. She reached out last week. Part of me wants to answer. Part of me is still furious.

Old Counsel

Two years is a long silence, and one message does not erase it. You don't have to decide the whole relationship tonight. You only have to decide whether this note gets a reply, and what kind.

You

If I reply, she'll think I've forgotten what she did.

Old Counsel

She can think that. You can't manage her story of you. You can be plain about what you're open to: a slow start, no pretence that nothing happened, and a pace you can stand. If you write, keep it short and true. If you're not ready, it's allowed to say you received it and you need time.

You

I've been offered a job in another city. I've had the offer for three weeks and I still haven't answered. I keep asking everyone else what I should do.

Old Counsel

Other people can tell you what they would do. They don't have to live the mornings that follow. The part that's yours is to name what you actually want, and what you're afraid of losing, without dressing either one up.

You

What if I choose wrong?

Old Counsel

Then you chose, and you'll deal with the next thing in front of you. Sitting on the offer is already a choice. It's the choice to stay, for now, without admitting it. Tonight, write two lists, only for yourself: what you'd be walking toward, and what you'd be walking away from. Then pick a day this week to answer.
